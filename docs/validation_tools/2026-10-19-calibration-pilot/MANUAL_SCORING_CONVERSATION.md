## User

okay bro, in simple steps, how is the manual scoring done with the teachers?

## Assistant

Bro, **each teacher scores the same worksheet separately, then they discuss differences to agree on the reference scores.**

1. **Review the rubric first.** Give both teachers the draft rubric and Form A. Collect their comments, revise unclear descriptions, and finalize one version before scoring.

2. **Practice together.** Use a few practice samples excluded from the calibration dataset. Discuss what each scoring band means.

3. **Prepare coded worksheet photos.** Label them with codes such as `S01-A`. Both teachers must see the same clear image.

4. **Score independently.** Each teacher records **five scores per worksheet**, using bands **1–4**:
   - Letter formation
   - Size consistency
   - Spacing
   - Slant uniformity
   - Baseline alignment

   They rate the worksheet as a whole using the rubric—not every word separately. They should not see each other’s ratings or the engine’s scores.

5. **Record uncertainty.** If a criterion cannot be judged, mark it **unable to rate** and explain why. Do not guess.

6. **Compare and discuss differences.** Preserve both original rating sheets. Then record their agreed score for each criterion on a separate agreement sheet. If they cannot agree, mark that criterion unresolved.

7. **Enter the agreed scores into WriteWise.** For a worksheet with all five scores agreed, the responsible teacher enters that final set. Keep the original paper ratings for checking teacher agreement.

8. **Use the paired data for calibration.** Your team matches those agreed teacher scores with the engine’s measurements to fit and evaluate preliminary scoring thresholds.

**Example:** Teacher 1 gives spacing **2**, while Teacher 2 gives **3**. They review the rubric and sample, agree on **2**, and record it—while keeping both original ratings.

## Assistant

**Yes, bro—you can visit Teacher 1 first, then Teacher 2.** They do not need to meet in person.

Follow this sequence:

1. **Have both review the draft rubric first.** Collect Form A separately from each teacher, combine their suggestions, and finalize one rubric version **before either starts actual scoring**.

2. **Orient them separately.** Give both the same instructions and practice examples so they understand the scoring bands consistently.

3. **Let Teacher 1 score independently.** Use coded worksheet photos and keep the completed ratings.

4. **Let Teacher 2 score the same photos independently.** Provide the same finalized rubric. **Do not show Teacher 1’s scores or the engine’s results.**

5. **Compare their ratings afterward.** Matching scores become agreed reference scores. For differences, ask each teacher to reconsider the specific sample and explain their rating. You can coordinate this through separate follow-ups or a shared call.

6. **Record the final outcome.** Preserve both original ratings and separately record any agreed scores. If they still disagree, mark the criterion **unresolved**—do not average the bands or choose a score yourselves.

The key is **the same rubric, the same images, and independent first ratings**. If the rubric changes after someone has scored, affected worksheets must be scored again using the revised version.
