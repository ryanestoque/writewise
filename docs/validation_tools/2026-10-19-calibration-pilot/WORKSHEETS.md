# Draft Copying Activities — October 19 Pilot

Status: draft prompt cards for teacher review. Freeze the exact text, line breaks, ruling format, and prompt version before collection. Use with [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) and the [teacher rubric](TEACHER_RUBRIC_AND_FORMS.md).

## Preparation

Prepare four separate core writing sheets A–D per child and two optional sheets E–F. Each has three visible groups of top/mid/baseline ruling. A Grade 3 handwriting pad describes a compatible ruling format; it does not restrict participants to Grade 3. Use the same ruling format across the sample where practical. Teachers must check that the line length fits the text comfortably before the version is frozen.

Present the prompt on a separate card or screen. Photograph only the child's writing and its rulings; printed model words and instructions must not appear in the segmentation region. Do not use dotted tracing letters on the writing sheet. Keep worksheet codes on the back or outside the captured writing area and match them to file/manifest codes immediately.

Teachers review familiar vocabulary, accepted cursive forms, and age suitability. The four activities collectively sample several common shape and stroke patterns, not every letter or all possible letter combinations. The same task/rubric is used across available grades; it is not a grade-normed assessment. All five criteria are rated on every page.

Before collection, create six app activities with the final approved target text, labeled A–F in the research manifest. Keep the target text exactly aligned with the copied content. A/B/E are intentionally word groups, not sentences; do not add printed titles or instructions to their target text. Punctuation is part of C/D/F. Preview how the actual pipeline handles it during rehearsal.

## Instructions read to each child

> We would like to see your usual cursive handwriting. Copy these words onto the matching three lines. Write at a comfortable pace. This is not a speed test. You can ask for a break or stop. If you make a mistake, make your usual correction and continue.

Read the prompt aloud if necessary to make the copying task accessible, and record that assistance. Do not demonstrate letter shapes or correct handwriting while the child works. Keep usual self-corrections. If a child cannot attempt cursive, record what happened rather than coaching a different performance for acceptance.

## A — Rounded shapes and common joins (core, 12 words)

Copy one row into each ruled writing group:

```text
am an at cat
can came name moon
noon room same home
```

Exact target text:

```text
am an at cat can came name moon noon room same home
```

Focus for text selection: common rounded forms, arches, repeated bodies, and joining patterns. Short words remain part of the target count even if a particular pipeline feature needs longer words.

## B — Tall letters and descenders (core, 12 words)

```text
big dog fish jump
play fly kite help
tall little yellow puppy
```

Exact target text:

```text
big dog fish jump play fly kite help tall little yellow puppy
```

Focus for text selection: mixed heights and letters extending below the baseline. Legitimate ascenders/descenders are not defects.

## C — Connected sentence copying (core, 13 words)

```text
The little cat sits near
my bag. We play after
class each day.
```

Exact target text:

```text
The little cat sits near my bag. We play after class each day.
```

Focus for text selection: connected writing, word separation, capitals, and baseline control across lines.

## D — Parallel sentence copying (core, 13 words)

```text
My puppy likes to play
outside. I give him food
and fresh water.
```

Exact target text:

```text
My puppy likes to play outside. I give him food and fresh water.
```

Focus for text selection: another sentence observation of comparable length, including tall letters, descenders, and repeated joins. Comparable word count does not prove equal difficulty; teachers review it.

## Order, breaks, and completion

Odd participant codes: A, B, break, C, D. Even codes: C, D, break, A, B. Offer another break before optional E, F. Additional breaks and stopping are always possible. Record the actual order and any deviations.

There are 50 core target words per child: 12 + 12 + 13 + 13. E–F add 25 optional words. Do not equate target word count with successfully written words or accepted model crops; record all three separately where available.

No speed score or fixed writing cutoff is imposed. Use the rehearsal to estimate a comfortable session length and shorten administrative waiting, not handwriting time. If a page is partial or declined, log that status; do not substitute a neater rewrite under the same worksheet code.
